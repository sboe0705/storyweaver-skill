# Genre Toolkits

Short guides for making each genre work interactively. Genres can be mixed freely (a sci-fi mystery, a fairy-tale horror) — combine the relevant tools.

## Fantasy
- **Wonder first.** Give magic a texture and a cost; describe one striking image per scene.
- **Lore as puzzle material.** Prophecies, runes, and legends make perfect riddle and cipher sources.
- **Choices:** loyalty vs. ambition, mercy vs. safety, which faction to trust.

## Science fiction
- **One big idea.** Build the story around one speculative concept (an AI that dreams, a generation ship, time loops) and explore its consequences.
- **Puzzles:** system logic, access codes, repairing machines with limited parts, decoding alien languages.
- **Choices:** progress vs. humanity, truth vs. stability.

## Mystery / detective
- **Fair play.** The culprit must be identifiable from the clues the user has collected. Introduce the culprit early.
- **Clue log.** Keep a visible 🔍 list of clues and testimonies; this is the core of the game.
- **Red herrings** are fine but must be resolvable.
- **Accusation scene.** Let the user name the culprit and explain their reasoning. Reward partial correctness. A wrong accusation should have consequences but can still lead to a (worse) ending.

## Horror
- **Dread over gore.** What is unseen is scarier. Use sound, silence, and small wrongnesses.
- **Escalation.** Start normal, introduce one odd detail, then let it grow.
- **Limited resources** (battery, matches, sanity) create tension through choices.
- **Calibrate** to the audience — "spooky" for kids, genuinely unsettling for adults who ask for it.

## Adventure / action
- **Momentum.** Short scenes, time pressure, clear goals.
- **Choices** about routes, risks, and gear. Physical puzzles and item use fit well.

## Romance / drama
- **Relationships are the state.** Track trust and affection; let dialogue choices matter.
- **Puzzles** are mostly social: understanding a character, finding the right words, uncovering a secret.

## Historical
- **Authentic texture** — period details in clothing, language, technology.
- **Puzzles** can draw on real historical knowledge, but always provide the necessary facts in-story.

## Fairy tales & stories for children
- **Gentle stakes.** Danger is mild, characters are kind or comically grumpy, everything resolves safely.
- **Short scenes** (~80–150 words), simple language, 2–3 clear choices.
- **Puzzles:** easy riddles, counting, colors, rhyming, "which one doesn't belong".
- **Praise effort.** Encourage on wrong answers; help proactively.
- **Bedtime mode:** calmer tone toward the end, a cozy, reassuring ending.

## Comedy
- **Absurd logic, internally consistent.** The world can be silly, but its rules must hold so puzzles stay fair.
- **Reward weird ideas** from the user with funny results.
